## What your dishes are telling you

Take a look at the last load before you call. What you see points to where the problem is.

You see

### Gritty residue or food bits

Water is recirculating dirty. The filter is clogged, or spray arm holes are blocked.

Clean the filter and rinse the spray arms. If it keeps happening, the wash pump may be weak.

You see

### Top rack dirty, bottom rack clean

Water is not reaching the upper spray arm, often from a low fill or a cracked or clogged arm.

Check that nothing blocks the arms. A low fill points to the inlet valve.

You see

### White, chalky spots or film

Hard water minerals drying on the dishes.

Use rinse aid and a dishwasher cleaner. A weak heating element can make it worse.

You see

### Cloudy glasses that will not wipe off

Etching from too much detergent in soft water, or scale buildup in hard water.

Try less detergent first. If the film wipes off with vinegar, it is scale.

You see

### Dishes soaking wet at the end

No heat during drying, or empty rinse aid on a condensation-dry model.

Fill the rinse aid. If they are still wet, the heating element or vent fan may have failed.

You see

### Detergent cup still closed

The dispenser latch is stuck or the cycle is not reaching the wash step.

Clean around the latch. If it still sticks, the dispenser or control needs a look.

## Dishwasher problems we repair

Tap the one that sounds like yours to see the usual cause.

### Water left in the bottom

Start with the filter under the lower spray arm. If it is clean, look at the drain hose under the sink, the garbage disposal connection, the air gap if you have one, and the drain pump.

Filter cleaning, hose fixes and drain pump replacement are routine.

### Dishes not getting clean

Clogged spray arms, a dirty filter, a low fill from a weak inlet valve, or a wash motor losing strength. Scale on the spray arm holes is a common culprit in hard water homes.

Most cleaning problems are solved with a thorough cleaning or a small part.

### Leaking under the door or onto the floor

A worn door gasket, a door that no longer closes square, regular dish soap making too many suds, or a leaking pump seal or inlet connection underneath the tub.

Gaskets and inlet connections are straightforward repairs.

### Won’t start or won’t fill

A door latch that does not register, a blown thermal fuse, a bad water inlet valve or a control board fault. Many models also have a float switch that stops the fill if it senses water in the base.

The technician tests each part before replacing anything.

### Dishes come out wet

A burned-out heating element or a failed vent fan, depending on the model. Newer dishwashers often dry by condensation and rely on rinse aid, so an empty dispenser makes a big difference.

Heating element replacement is a straightforward repair.

### Grinding, humming or loud noise

Broken glass, a seed or a fruit pit in the chopper or drain pump, or a wash motor bearing wearing out.

Clearing debris often fixes it. Worn motors can be replaced.

Quick answer

## Water in the bottom? Check these first

- 1 Turn off the dishwasher and let the water cool. Lay a towel in front of the door.
- 2 Twist out the filter under the lower spray arm and rinse it under the tap. This fixes a lot of drainage complaints.
- 3 If the dishwasher drains into a garbage disposal, run the disposal. A newly installed disposal may still have its knockout plug in place.
- 4 Look under the sink. The drain hose should loop up high under the counter with no kinks or sags. If there is a small air gap cap on the sink deck, pull it off and clear it.
- 5 Run a short rinse cycle. If it hums at the end and still will not drain, the drain pump is the likely cause.
Watch for broken glass in the filter and the sump, and turn off the breaker before reaching near the pump.

## The drain hose, the high loop and the air gap

A dishwasher drain hose has to rise above the sink drain before it connects, so dirty sink water cannot flow back into the dishwasher. That is done with a high loop, where the hose is fastened up under the countertop, or with an air gap fitting on the sink deck.

Which one your home needs depends on the plumbing code your city has adopted and the dishwasher maker’s instructions. Installation manuals usually set a minimum height for the loop, often around 18 to 20 inches above the floor depending on the brand. When a dishwasher keeps backing up or smells, a hose that has slipped down from that loop is one of the first things we check.

## How often to clean the filter

Manufacturers set the schedule by how you load. Whirlpool, for example, suggests cleaning about twice a year for light use with scraped dishes, every three months for 8 to 14 loads a week, and much more often if dishes go in unscraped. Bosch suggests 3 to 6 times a year for light to average use. Check your manual, and clean it sooner after loads heavy with starch, seeds or broken glass.

## Repair or replace your dishwasher?

Dishwashers usually last about 9 to 12 years. A drain pump, inlet valve, latch, seal or heating element repair is almost always worth doing. A failed control board plus a tired wash motor on an older, basic unit is where replacement starts to compete.

Remember that replacing a built-in dishwasher also means removal, installation and plumbing and electrical hookups, which is part of why repair often wins.

- Clogs, valves, latches, seals and elements: repair
- Rusted tub or racks plus a failed board: consider replacement
- Mid-range and premium models: repair usually makes more sense

## Dishwasher repair questions

### Is some water in the bottom of my dishwasher normal?

A small amount of water down in the sump, under the filter, is normal on many models. It keeps the seals from drying out. Water covering the tub floor after a cycle is not normal.

### Does hard water damage dishwashers?

Over time, yes. Mineral scale builds up on spray arms, the inlet valve screen and the heating element. Rinse aid and a dishwasher cleaner every month or two help keep it under control.

### My dishwasher only leaks sometimes. Can you find the cause?

Yes. Intermittent leaks are often a door gasket, suds from the wrong detergent, or a pump seal that leaks only during part of the cycle. The technician runs it and watches where the water appears.

### Do I need to empty the dishwasher before the technician arrives?

Please do, and clear out the cabinet under the sink. It lets the technician reach the filter, spray arms and drain connections right away.

### Can I use drain cleaner in my dishwasher?

No. Chemical drain cleaners can damage the pump seals and plastic parts. If the dishwasher will not drain, clean the filter and check the hose and disposal, then call.

### How much does dishwasher repair cost?

It depends on the model and the part, so we do not list prices online. Call to discuss your repair needs, with the brand and model number ready.

### Where is the model number on my dishwasher?

Open the door and look along the top or side edge of the door, or on the tub frame just inside. A photo of the label works.
