## Freezer warming up? Protect the food first

- 1 Keep the door or lid shut. A full freezer holds a safe temperature for about 48 hours, a half-full one about 24.
- 2 Put an appliance thermometer inside. A working freezer should read 0°F.
- 3 If it is climbing fast, move the most valuable items to a cooler with ice or to another freezer.
- 4 Check that the plug is in, the breaker is on and nothing is blocking the coils or vents.
- 5 Call us with the brand, the model number and whether it is a chest or an upright.
Do not try to speed up a defrost with a knife, ice pick or heat gun. Puncturing a cooling line ruins the freezer.

## Can I refreeze it?

Food safety guidance from the USDA and university extension services, for food from a freezer that warmed up.

| Food | Safe to refreeze when | Throw it out when |
|---|---|---|
| Meat and poultry Safe to refreeze when: It still has ice crystals or has stayed at 40°F or below | It still has ice crystals or has stayed at 40°F or below | It sat above 40°F for more than two hours |
| Vegetables and fruit Safe to refreeze when: Ice crystals are still present | Ice crystals are still present | It warmed above 40°F, or shows mold or a sour smell |
| Shellfish and cooked dishes Safe to refreeze when: Ice crystals are still present | Ice crystals are still present | It warmed above 40°F |
| Ice cream and frozen desserts Safe to refreeze when: Still frozen solid | Still frozen solid | It partly thawed, even if it is cold |

Refreezing can lower the quality of some foods, but it stays safe under these conditions. Appearance and smell are not reliable tests. When in doubt, throw it out.

## Chest and upright freezers fail differently

The type of freezer you have changes what usually goes wrong.

### Chest freezers

- Usually manual defrost, so frost builds up over time
- Lid gaskets that crack or no longer seal at the corners
- Start relays and overloads on the compressor
- Condenser coils inside the walls, so airflow around the cabinet matters
- Drain plugs that leak melt water during defrosting
### Upright freezers

- Often frost-free, with a defrost heater, thermostat and control
- Evaporator fan motors that stop or freeze up
- Door gaskets and door alignment
- Defrost drains that clog and leak
- Thermostats and temperature sensors that drift

## Freezer problems and their usual causes

Tap the one that sounds like yours to see the usual cause.

### Not freezing

Dirty condenser coils, a failed condenser fan, a bad start relay or thermostat, or a compressor problem. Frost-free uprights can also fail in the defrost system.

Relays, thermostats and fans are routine repairs.

### Heavy frost or ice buildup

On chest and manual-defrost freezers, frost builds slowly by design but piles up fast with a bad lid seal. On frost-free uprights, heavy frost means the defrost heater, thermostat or control has failed.

Gaskets and defrost parts are replaceable.

### Running all the time

A hot garage, blocked airflow around the coils, a leaking seal or a low refrigerant charge. A freezer packed tight against the walls can run longer too.

Many causes are fixed by cleaning, better airflow or a new seal.

### Clicking but not running

The compressor is trying to start and failing. Often it is the start relay or overload, and sometimes the compressor itself.

Relay replacement is quick.

### Water under or around the freezer

Condensation from a poor lid or door seal, a clogged defrost drain on an upright, or melt water a chest freezer drain plug did not hold.

Seal replacement or drain clearing.

### Temperature alarm going off

Many freezers alarm when the inside temperature climbs or the door is left ajar. The cause is either a cooling problem or a seal problem.

The technician traces why the temperature climbed.

### Loud buzzing or rattling

A fan blade hitting ice, a failing fan motor, or a compressor that is struggling. Rattles can also come from a freezer that is not level.

Fan motors and leveling are simple fixes.

## Garage freezers in a Texas summer

Plenty of Temple households keep a second freezer in the garage. Every freezer is designed for a certain room temperature range, listed in the owner’s manual. Garage-ready models are built for wider swings. Some manufacturers rate them for rooms from 0°F to 110°F, while many standard freezers are made for indoor temperatures. A closed Central Texas garage in July can reach the top of that range.

If yours lives in the garage, give it a few inches of space on every side, keep it out of direct afternoon sun, and vacuum the coils or vents a couple of times a year. A freezer that runs nonstop every summer is wearing out its compressor faster.

Before a visit, move anything stacked on top of or against the freezer, and make sure the technician can reach the outlet and the back of the unit. In a shop or barn, clear a dry spot to work.

Safety

## Unplug it and call if

- You smell burning or see scorch marks near the compressor area.
- The freezer trips the breaker or the outlet feels hot.
- Water is pooling near the plug or the outlet.
- The compressor clicks every few minutes and never runs.
- You see oily residue on the tubing, which can point to a refrigerant leak.
Refrigerant work requires EPA Section 608 certification. Never cut or puncture freezer tubing.

## Is your freezer worth repairing?

Freezers often last 10 to 20 years, longer than most appliances. Relays, thermostats, fans, defrost parts and seals are worth fixing at almost any age.

A sealed system failure (compressor or refrigerant leak) is different. On a small or older chest freezer, replacement usually makes more sense. On a large upright or a built-in freezer column, repair often still wins. The technician will give you an honest read.

- Relay, fan, thermostat, defrost part or seal: repair
- Compressor failure on a small or older chest freezer: replacement usually makes more sense
- Large uprights and built-in columns: repair usually pays off

## Freezer repair questions

### How long will food stay frozen if my freezer stops?

About 48 hours for a full freezer and about 24 hours for a half-full one, as long as the door or lid stays closed.

### Can I refreeze food that started to thaw?

If the food still has ice crystals or has stayed at 40°F or below, it can generally be refrozen, though quality may drop. Throw out partly thawed ice cream, and anything that sat above 40°F for more than two hours.

### Do you repair chest freezers?

Yes, along with upright freezers. Tell us which type and brand you have when you call.

### Why is my freezer covered in frost?

On a frost-free upright, heavy frost means a defrost part failed. On a chest or manual-defrost freezer, it usually points to a worn seal letting humid air in, or the lid being opened often.

### Do you come out to freezers in garages and shops?

Yes. Garage and shop freezers are a common call in Temple. Make sure there is room around the unit for the technician to reach the back and the coils.

### Should I defrost my chest freezer before the technician comes?

No. Leave it as it is unless we ask. The frost pattern and the temperature help the technician see what is going on. Just clear a path to the freezer and make room around it.

### Where is the model number on my freezer?

On chest freezers, look on the back or the side near the bottom, or under the lid. On uprights, check the inside wall near the top or the door frame. A photo of the label works.

### How much does freezer repair cost?

It depends on the freezer and the part, so we do not list prices online. Call to discuss your repair needs, with the brand, model number and type of freezer ready.
