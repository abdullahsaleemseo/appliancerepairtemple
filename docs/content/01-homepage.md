### H2: Common appliance problems we fix in Temple.

These are the calls we get most. Each answer covers the usual causes, so you know what you are dealing with before the technician arrives.

- **Refrigerator not cooling.** Usually a stopped fan, a frosted evaporator coil or a start relay that is failing. Keep the doors shut while you wait. A closed fridge holds a safe temperature for about four hours.
- **Washer not draining.** A clogged pump filter, a kinked drain hose or a drain pump that quit. Turn the washer off and unplug it before you open the filter door, and have towels ready.
- **Dryer not heating.** On electric dryers, often a heating element or thermal fuse. On gas dryers, an igniter or gas valve coil. A clogged vent is a common root cause, so airflow gets checked too.
- **Dishwasher leaving water or dirty dishes.** A packed filter, clogged spray arms, a bad inlet valve or a drain pump problem. Mineral buildup from hard water can make all of these worse over time.
- **Oven not heating or baking unevenly.** A burned-out bake element on electric models, a weak igniter on gas models, or a temperature sensor that has drifted away from the setting.
- **Freezer frosting up or running warm.** A defrost part, a worn door or lid seal, or dirty condenser coils. Garage freezers feel this most in a Central Texas summer.
- **Ice maker stopped making ice.** A frozen fill tube, a failed water inlet valve, a clogged filter or a worn ice maker module. Check that the shutoff arm is down first.
- **Error code on the display.** A code points to a system, such as draining or the door lock, not always to one part. Write it down before you call. It helps the technician plan the visit.

**When to stop using the appliance:** Turn it off and call if you see smoke or sparks, smell burning, or find water pooling near wiring or an outlet. If you smell gas near a range or dryer, leave the house first and call your gas utility from outside.

### H2: How scheduling and repair service works.

Four steps from the first call to an appliance that runs again.

1. **Call or send a request.** Tell us the appliance, the brand and what it is doing. A photo of the model number label helps.
2. **We confirm the visit.** We call to confirm the details and set a visit window. We are open Monday to Saturday, 8:00 AM to 8:00 PM.
3. **Diagnosis at your home.** The technician checks the appliance, tests the likely parts, and explains what failed and what the repair involves.
4. **Repair and test.** With your OK, the technician makes the repair and runs the appliance to confirm it works.

## 5. FAQs (FAQPage markup, visible on the page)

1. **What appliances do you repair in Temple?** Refrigerators, freezers, washers, dryers, dishwashers, ovens, ranges, cooktops and over-the-range microwaves. If yours is not on that list, call and ask before you rule it out.
2. **Do you offer same-day appliance repair?** Yes. Same-day service is available, subject to technician availability, location, and current demand. Calling earlier in the day gives you a better shot at a same-day visit.
3. **How fast do you respond?** Our typical response is about 60 minutes during business hours. It depends on technician availability, your location and how busy the day is, so treat it as a typical figure, not a guaranteed arrival time.
4. **What are your hours?** Monday through Saturday, 8:00 AM to 8:00 PM. Requests sent outside those hours are answered when we reopen.
5. **How much will my repair cost?** We do not list prices online because every repair depends on the appliance, the brand, the failed part and the problem. Call to discuss your repair needs. Having the model number and a short description of the problem ready makes that call more useful.
6. **Do you offer payment options?** Yes. Flexible payment options are available. Call for service details and we will walk you through them.
7. **Are you licensed and insured?** Yes. Appliance Repair Temple is licensed and insured.
8. **Should I repair my appliance or replace it?** Look at age and condition. A newer appliance with one failed part, such as a fan, pump, igniter or valve, is usually worth repairing. An older unit with a major failure, such as a compressor or transmission, deserves a closer look. The technician will explain the options so you can decide.
