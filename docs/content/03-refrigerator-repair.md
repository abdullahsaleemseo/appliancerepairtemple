### H2: Fridge warm right now? Do this first.

1. Keep the doors closed. A closed refrigerator keeps food cold for about 4 hours. A full freezer holds for about 48 hours, a half-full one about 24.
2. Check the basics: the plug, the breaker and the temperature setting. Dials get bumped during cleaning more often than you would think.
3. Put an appliance thermometer on a middle shelf. The fresh-food side should read 40°F or below and the freezer 0°F.
4. Listen. Is the compressor humming? Does a fan run inside the freezer when you hold the door switch in?
5. Call us with the brand, the model number and what you noticed.

> Throw out perishables such as milk, meat and leftovers that sat above 40°F for more than two hours. When in doubt, throw it out.

### H2: Refrigerator symptoms and what they usually mean

- **Not cooling at all.** Light on but nothing running? Look at the start relay, overload, control board or compressor. Compressor running but both sides warm? The condenser coils may be packed with dust or the condenser fan may have stopped. *Relays, fans and coil cleaning are routine. Compressor work is a bigger decision we talk through with you.*
- **Fridge warm, freezer still cold.** Cold air is made in the freezer and pushed into the fridge. A frosted evaporator coil, a stopped evaporator fan or a stuck air damper keeps the freezer cold while the fridge warms up. *One of the most common refrigerator repairs we do.*
- **Freezer warm or frosting up.** Thick frost on the freezer back panel points to the defrost heater, thermostat or control. Soft ice cream with no frost points to the fan, relay or compressor. *Defrost system repairs are routine on most models.*
- **Water leaking inside or on the floor.** Water under the crisper drawers usually means a clogged defrost drain. Water on the floor can come from the same drain, a cracked ice maker line, a loose filter housing or a leaking inlet valve. *Drain clearing and valve or line replacement are usually one-visit jobs.*
- **Ice maker or water dispenser not working.** Check that the ice maker arm is down and the supply valve is open. Past that, it is usually a frozen fill tube, a failed inlet valve, an old filter or the ice maker module. *Inlet valves and ice maker modules are standard parts for most brands.*
- **Loud buzzing, clicking or squealing.** Squealing that gets louder with the freezer door open is usually the evaporator fan. Rattling underneath is often the condenser fan or drain pan. Clicking every few minutes means the compressor is trying to start and failing. *Fan motors and relays are quick replacements once the noise is traced.*
- **Running all the time.** A fridge that never shuts off is fighting to keep up: dirty coils, a torn door gasket, a failing fan or low refrigerant. In a hot garage, the room may be warmer than the unit was built for. *Gaskets, fans and a coil cleaning fix most of these.*
- **Error code or blinking display.** A code narrows the search to a system, such as a sensor or fan circuit, but rarely names the exact part. The technician confirms it with a meter before ordering anything. *Sensor and control faults are common and fixable on most models.*

### H2: The parts behind most refrigerator repairs

Knowing what each part does makes the diagnosis easier to follow.

| Part | What it does | Signs it is failing |
|---|---|---|
| Evaporator fan | Moves cold air from the freezer coil into both sections | Fridge warm while the freezer stays cold, or a squeal that gets louder with the freezer door open |
| Condenser fan and coils | Release heat from under or behind the unit | Both sides warm, the fridge runs nonstop, no warm air moving under the unit |
| Defrost heater, thermostat and control | Melt frost off the evaporator coil several times a day | Thick frost on the freezer back panel and a fridge that warms up slowly |
| Start relay and overload | Get the compressor started | Clicking every few minutes while the compressor never kicks on |
| Compressor | Pumps refrigerant through the sealed system | Runs but does not cool, or will not run once the relay is ruled out |
| Door gasket | Seals the cold air in | Sweating or frost at the door edge, and a fridge that runs constantly |
| Water inlet valve | Lets water into the ice maker and dispenser | No ice, a slow dispenser, or water on the floor behind the fridge |

### H2: When to unplug it and call a technician

Most refrigerator problems can wait a few hours. These cannot.

- You smell burning or see scorch marks near the back or bottom of the unit.
- The refrigerator trips the breaker, or the plug or outlet feels hot.
- Water is pooling near the plug, the outlet or the compressor area.
- The compressor clicks on and off every few minutes and never runs. Unplugging it protects the compressor until it is checked.
- You see oily residue on the tubing or smell a chemical odor, which can point to a refrigerant leak.

Refrigerant work requires EPA Section 608 certification. Never cut, bend or puncture refrigerator tubing, and do not chip ice off the coils with a sharp tool.

### H2: What you can check, and what to leave to us

**Safe to check yourself:** clean the condenser coils with a coil brush or vacuum, with the fridge unplugged · check that the doors close fully and the gaskets are clean · replace an old water filter if the dispenser has slowed down · level the refrigerator so the doors swing shut on their own.

**Leave to a technician:** testing relays, fans, sensors and boards with a meter · anything behind the freezer back panel, including defrost parts · water valve and supply line replacement behind the unit · compressor, refrigerant and sealed system work.

### H2: How a refrigerator repair visit works

1. **Temperatures and a listen.** The technician checks the temperature in both sections and listens to the compressor and fans.
2. **Look behind the panels.** When needed, the freezer back panel comes off so the coil, fan and defrost parts can be checked for frost and wear.
3. **Test, do not guess.** Suspect parts are tested with a meter before anything is replaced. You hear what failed and what the repair involves.
4. **Repair and confirm.** After your OK, the part is replaced and cooling is confirmed. A fridge can take several hours to get fully back to temperature.

### H2: Is your refrigerator worth repairing?

Most refrigerators last 10 to 15 years. A useful rule of thumb: if the repair would cost more than half of what a comparable new refrigerator costs, and your unit is near the end of that range, replacement starts to make sense.

Fans, defrost parts, relays, valves and gaskets are almost always worth repairing. Compressor or refrigerant work on an older fridge deserves a harder look. The technician gives you an honest read; the decision stays yours.

- Under about 8 years old: repair is usually the right call
- Sealed system failure on a 12+ year old unit: compare against a new refrigerator too
- Built-in and counter-depth models: repair usually makes more sense, since replacing them can mean cabinet work

## 5. FAQs (FAQPage markup)

1. **How long will food last in a refrigerator that stopped cooling?** Keep the doors closed. A closed refrigerator keeps food safe for about 4 hours. A full freezer holds for about 48 hours and a half-full one for about 24. Throw out perishables such as milk, meat and leftovers that sat above 40°F for more than two hours.
2. **Can my refrigerator be repaired the same day?** Same-day service is available, subject to technician availability, location, and current demand. Many common refrigerator parts can be replaced on the first visit. Some model-specific parts have to be ordered, and the technician will tell you if that is the case.
3. **Do you repair refrigerator compressors and refrigerant leaks?** Sealed system repairs are possible on many models, but they are bigger jobs and refrigerant must be handled by an EPA-certified technician. The technician confirms the diagnosis and talks through whether the repair makes sense for your refrigerator before anything is done.
4. **Is it OK to keep a refrigerator in a Temple garage?** Check your owner's manual for the room temperature range your model is designed for. Many standard refrigerators are built for indoor temperatures, and a Texas garage in July can run hotter than that. Garage-ready models, shade and space around the coils all help.
5. **How much does refrigerator repair cost?** It depends on the model and the part that failed, so we do not list prices online. Call to discuss your repair needs. Have the brand, the model number and a short description of the problem ready.
6. **Where do I find my refrigerator's model number?** Look for a label inside the fresh-food section, usually on a side wall or the ceiling near the top. Some models put it behind the crisper drawers or on the back. A photo of the label works.
