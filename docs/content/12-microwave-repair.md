It decides whether repair makes sense at all.

- Mounted to the wall and cabinet, or set into cabinetry
- Replacement means removal, mounting and fit work

- It sparks, arcs or flashes inside with nothing metal in it.
- The door, hinges, latch or seal are damaged, or the door does not close properly.
- It keeps running after the door is opened.
- You smell burning or see smoke from the vents or the cabinet.
- The breaker trips when it starts heating.

## Why microwave repair is not a do-it-yourself job

To make heat, a microwave steps household current up to thousands of volts and stores some of it in a high-voltage capacitor. Manufacturer service manuals tell technicians to unplug the unit, wait, and then discharge that capacitor with an insulated tool before touching anything inside. Repair guides warn the charge can linger well beyond that.

That is why the outer cabinet should stay closed unless you are trained to work on it. The checks below are safe. Anything past them is a job for a technician.

## Microwave problems we repair

Tap the one that sounds like yours to see the usual cause.

### Runs but does not heat

The turntable spins and the light is on, but food stays cold. The usual causes are a failed magnetron, high-voltage diode or capacitor, or a door switch that is not engaging correctly.

Fixable on most over-the-range and built-in units. Leave this one to a technician.

### Completely dead, no display

A blown internal fuse is common, and a failing door switch is often what blew it. A control board fault can also leave the display dark.

Fuse and door switch replacement are routine.

### Sparking or arcing inside

Metal in the cavity, a burned or greasy waveguide cover, or chipped interior paint that exposes bare metal. Stop using it until it is checked.

Waveguide covers are simple to replace. Large areas of damaged paint may mean replacement.

### Keypad not responding

A worn membrane keypad, moisture behind it from steam, or a control board problem.

Keypads and boards are replaceable on many models.

### Turntable not turning

A failed turntable motor, a cracked coupler under the glass tray, or a tray that is not seated on its roller ring.

Motor or coupler replacement.

### Vent fan or light not working

On over-the-range units: a failed fan motor, a burned-out bulb or socket, or the switch for the fan or light.

Standard parts on most brands.

### Loud hum or buzzing while heating

Some hum is normal. A loud or changing buzz can come from a failing magnetron, a high-voltage diode or a cooling fan rubbing.

The technician finds the source before replacing anything.

## Safe checks before you call

- 1 Check the outlet and the breaker. Over-the-range units are often on their own circuit.
- 2 Open and close the door firmly. If it does not latch cleanly, stop and call.
- 3 Heat a cup of water for one minute. If it is barely warm, the heating circuit has a problem.
- 4 On over-the-range models, check whether the vent fan and light still work. It helps narrow the fault.
- 5 Make sure the glass tray is seated on its roller ring and coupler.

## Over-the-range extras: the vent fan, light and filters

An over-the-range microwave doubles as the range hood. Its grease filters sit under the unit and should be cleaned regularly, more often if you fry or sear a lot. Models that recirculate air instead of venting outside also use a charcoal filter that needs replacing from time to time. A clogged filter makes the fan work harder and can make it noisy.

Before a visit, clear the cooktop below the microwave and the cabinet shelf above it. Many over-the-range repairs mean taking the unit off its mounting plate, and the technician needs room to support it safely.

## Repair or replace your microwave?

For countertop microwaves, replacing usually makes more sense than a service visit. For over-the-range and built-in units, the math changes: a replacement means removing the old unit, mounting the new one and sometimes matching the cabinet opening, so repairing a door switch, fuse, turntable or fan is usually worth it.

A magnetron failure on an older over-the-range unit is the closer call. It depends on the age of the microwave and whether the part is still available. The technician will tell you plainly.

- Countertop microwave: replacement usually makes more sense

## Microwave repair questions

### Is it worth repairing a microwave?

For over-the-range and built-in models, often yes, because replacing them means removal and installation work too. For countertop units, replacement is usually the simpler choice.

### Why does my microwave run but not heat?

The usual causes are a door switch, high-voltage diode, capacitor or magnetron. These are high-voltage parts and should only be tested by a technician.

### Is it safe to use a microwave with a damaged door?

No. If the door, hinges, latch or seal are damaged, or the door does not close properly, stop using it until it has been checked.

### Can you replace the light or vent fan in my over-the-range microwave?

Yes. Fan motors, bulbs, sockets and switches are standard parts on most over-the-range models.

### Do you install over-the-range microwaves?

Repair is our main service. Call and ask about installation for your situation.

### Why is my over-the-range microwave fan so loud?

Grease-clogged filters make the fan work harder. Clean the filters first. If it still rattles, squeals or grinds, the fan motor or a blade may be failing, and both can be replaced.

### How much does microwave repair cost?

It depends on the model and the part, so we do not list prices online. Call to discuss your repair needs with the brand and model number ready.

### Where is the model number on my microwave?

Open the door and look on the frame around the opening or on the inside of the door. On over-the-range units it can also be behind the vent grille at the top front.
