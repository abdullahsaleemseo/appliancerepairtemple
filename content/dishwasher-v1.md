## What's on the dishes tells you what's wrong

| What's on the dishes | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| White, chalky film or spots | Hard water minerals, often with no rinse aid | Use rinse aid, try a dishwasher cleaner, and check the detergent dose for hard water |
| Gritty bits or food specks | Clogged filter or spray arm holes | Clean the filter; clear spray arm holes with a toothpick |
| Cloudy film that won't wipe or soak off | Etching from soft water and too much detergent | Cut back the detergent; etching on glass is permanent |
| Food baked on after the cycle | Water not hot enough, or a weak wash pump | Run the kitchen tap hot first; if it persists, the heater or pump needs testing |
| Dishes wet at the end, especially plastics | Normal on many condensation-dry models, or no rinse aid | Add rinse aid; use the heated or extra dry option if there is one |

Chalky film you can scrub off with vinegar is minerals; cloudy film that stays is etching, and etching doesn't come back out. If the dishes keep coming out dirty after the filter and spray arms are clean, the dishwasher not cleaning repair is usually the wash pump, the heater or a spray arm that no longer turns.

## Follow the water: in, around, out

Every dishwasher problem lives somewhere on this one path.

**Getting water in.** Water comes from a small supply valve under the sink, through a line to the inlet valve at the bottom front of the dishwasher. A float switch in the tub tells the control when enough water is in. If the tub stays dry or barely wet, the trouble is here.

**Moving it around.** The wash pump pushes water up through the spray arms, and a heater, on most models, warms it. The filter catches food so the pump keeps recirculating clean water. Poor cleaning with a normal fill points to this stage.

**Getting it out.** At the end of each wash and rinse, the drain pump pushes water through the drain hose, up to a high loop or an air gap, and into the sink drain or garbage disposal. Dirty water sitting in the tub afterward means something on this stretch is blocked or has quit.

## Why is my dishwasher not draining?

Start inside. The filter in the tub floor is the most common clog, especially on newer models that rely on a manual filter instead of a grinder. Twist it out and rinse it under the tap.

Then follow the drain hose. It should rise in a high loop under the counter, or run to an air gap on the sink deck, so dirty sink water can't flow back in. A hose that sags to the floor or gets crushed behind the dishwasher can hold water or kink shut. An air gap clogged with food will also back up, and sometimes overflow at the sink.

Next is the connection to the sink drain. If the dishwasher drains into a garbage disposal, a clogged disposal blocks it, and a newly installed disposal still has a knockout plug sealing its dishwasher inlet until someone punches it out. If the plug or the disposal itself looks like the culprit, [garbage disposal repair](https://appliancerepairtemple.com/garbage-disposal-repair-temple-tx/) covers jams, the knockout plug and where a disposal ends and the dishwasher begins.

Only after all that does the drain pump come into question: a hum with no water moving means it's jammed or failing. If you'd rather work through the filter, hose and disposal yourself first, the [dishwasher drainage problems you can check yourself](https://appliancerepairtemple.com/blog/dishwasher-drainage-problems/) follow roughly that order. Most dishwasher not draining repair calls end at the filter, the hose or the disposal, which is good news for the bill.

## Dishwasher not filling with water?

First, the obvious one that gets missed: the small supply valve under the sink. It gets bumped closed more often than you'd think. Make sure it's fully open.

If the valve is open and the tub stays dry, the inlet valve at the bottom of the dishwasher may have failed, or its small screen may be clogged with sediment. A float switch stuck in the "up" position also tells the control the tub is already full; on many models, the float is a small dome in the tub floor you can lift and lower by hand to free it. A door that isn't latching fully will stop the cycle before it fills, too.

## Dishwasher leaking under the door or from underneath

Where the water shows up says a lot. At the bottom of the door, the usual causes are a worn or torn door gasket, a dishwasher that isn't level so water runs toward the door, or regular dish soap squirted in by mistake, which turns into a flood of suds.

A spray arm knocking a tall pan can throw water at the door seal. From underneath, a leak points to a hose connection, the inlet valve or a worn pump or tub seal, and those can soak the cabinet floor before you notice. For dishwasher leaking repair in Temple, take a quick look under the kick plate with a flashlight while it runs.

Built-in dishwasher repair also means checking how it's mounted. A dishwasher has to be level side to side and front to back, and secured to the countertop or cabinet sides with its mounting brackets. One that's loose or tilted can leak at the door and wear its hinges early.

## Error codes, and what Bosch's E15 means

Many dishwashers display a code or flash a light pattern when something fails, and the code names the stage of the water path. Bosch is a good example. On a Bosch, E15 means water has been detected in the base pan under the tub, and the anti-flood float has shut the dishwasher down. It won't run again until the leak is found and the base is dry, and [Bosch dishwasher codes and quirks](https://appliancerepairtemple.com/brands/bosch-appliance-repair-temple-tx/) like E24 and E25, which usually point to drain blockages on many models, are worth knowing before you call. Bosch also dries by condensation on many models rather than with an exposed heating element, which is why plastics often come out damp and rinse aid matters so much. ## What happens on a dishwasher visit

The first few minutes usually go to the filter and the sump, the low point in the tub where water collects, because a clog there explains so many symptoms. The technician looks for food, glass and labels that have slipped past the filter, and checks that the float moves freely. Then a test cycle runs while each phase is watched: does water come in at a normal rate, do the spray arms turn and spray evenly, does the water heat, and does the pump drain the tub completely at the end. Whichever phase misbehaves points to the part.

## Dishwasher repair FAQ

### Should I run the kitchen tap before starting the dishwasher?

Yes. Run the hot water at the kitchen sink until it's hot, then start the dishwasher. Many dishwashers fill with whatever is in the line first, and in a house where the water heater is far from the kitchen, that's cold water. Hotter fill water cleans better.

### Can I use regular dish soap in a dishwasher?

No. Hand dish soap makes a huge amount of suds in a dishwasher, which can push foam out of the door and onto the floor and trip leak sensors. If it happens, stop the cycle, scoop out the suds, add a little cooking oil to cut them and run a rinse.

### How often should I clean the dishwasher filter?

Once a month is a good habit for a family that runs it daily, and more often if dishes go in with a lot of food left on them. Manual filters twist out from the bottom of the tub. Rinse under warm water with a soft brush, and don't use wire brushes on the mesh.

### Is it normal for water to stay in the bottom after a cycle?

A small amount of clear water around the sump is normal on many models; it keeps the seals from drying out. Dirty water above the filter, or water that covers the tub floor, isn't. That points to a clog or a drain problem.

### Why does my dishwasher smell bad?

Food trapped in the filter and the sump rots between cycles. Clean the filter, wipe the door gasket and the bottom edge of the door, and run an empty hot cycle with a dishwasher cleaner. Leaving the door slightly open after a cycle helps it dry out.
