## Propane country

Rogers sits out US-190 beyond Temple's eastern edge, where natural gas mains don't always reach. Plenty of homes in and around town run their gas range, cooktop, dryer or water heater on propane, also called LP, from a tank on the property. The appliances look the same as natural gas models, and many are the exact same model. What's different is inside: the burner orifices, the regulator setting and the pressure the appliance is built to use.

That difference is where trouble starts. A range moved from a house in town, or a dryer bought new and installed without its conversion, can run on the wrong setup for years, burning dirty, heating slowly or tripping its safety controls. Knowing what you have is half the diagnosis.

## Propane vs natural gas: what's different

| Topic | Natural gas | Propane (LP) |
|---|---|---|
| Supply | Piped in from a utility through a meter | Stored in a tank on your property and filled by your propane company |
| Pressure at the appliance | Lower, around 3.5 to 7 inches of water column | Higher, around 10 to 11 inches of water column |
| Conversion | Most ranges and dryers ship set up for natural gas | Needs the maker's LP conversion: smaller orifices and the regulator switched to LP |
| What a good flame looks like | Steady blue with a small, defined inner cone | The same: steady and blue, tips maybe faintly yellow |
| Wrong setup symptoms | Natural gas through LP orifices: weak, small flames, slow oven, burners hard to light | LP through natural gas orifices: tall yellow flames, soot on pans and walls, a sharp smell, a carbon monoxide risk |
| If it leaks | Lighter than air, rises | Heavier than air, settles low near the floor |
| Who handles the supply | The gas utility | Your propane company handles the tank, regulator and supply piping |

## Propane ranges and cooktops

Most propane range calls come down to flame and ignition. Burners that click but won't light can be a dirty igniter or a cap that's off center. Tall, yellow, wavering flames usually mean the conversion wasn't done or was done halfway, with the surface burners converted but the oven left on natural gas orifices, or the regulator never flipped. That's not a "keep using it" situation: dirty flames make carbon monoxide. Oven-side faults, a weak igniter, a bad thermostat, a valve that won't open, are tested the same way on LP as on natural gas, as laid out in [how gas and electric ovens get diagnosed](https://appliancerepairtemple.com/oven-stove-repair-temple-tx/).

## Propane dryers

A gas dryer on propane that tumbles but won't heat often has the same culprits as one on natural gas: a worn igniter, failed gas valve coils or a blown thermal fuse from a clogged vent. If the dryer has never heated well since it was installed, the conversion is the first suspect. Clothes that come out smelling of fuel point to an incomplete burn. ## Rogers FAQ

### Can a natural gas dryer be switched to propane?

Usually, yes, if the maker offers an LP conversion kit for that model. The kit swaps the orifice and changes the gas valve setting. Once it's done, the dryer should be labeled as converted so the next person knows.

### Why is my propane range flame yellow?

A mostly yellow, lazy flame on propane usually means the burner is getting too much gas for its orifice, often from an incomplete conversion, or that the burner cap or air shutter is out of place. Turn it off, open a window, and have it checked before using it again.
