## Why does the garage freezer quit in July, and sometimes in January?

Every freezer is designed for a range of room temperatures, and many standard models expect a climate-controlled house. A Temple garage in July can sit well above 100°F in the afternoon, which pushes an indoor-rated freezer past what it was built for. Parts like the start relay and condenser fan wear out sooner. The rating sticker inside the lid or door, or the manual, shows the range for your model. Freezers sold as "garage ready" handle wider swings.

Airflow makes that heat better or worse. A chest freezer shoved against the back wall, boxed in by a toolbox and a stack of paint cans, has nowhere to dump heat. Give it a few inches of space.

Winter has its own catch. In a hard freeze, some freezers in an unheated garage stop running because the thermostat senses the cold room and decides there's nothing to do. A freezer that acts up only in a cold snap is telling you about its rating, not a broken part.

Out past the I-35 corridor, a freezer in an uninsulated barn or metal shop gets both extremes. If a garage freezer is not working in summer, look at where it sits before assuming the worst.

## Why is my freezer not freezing?

First, does the compressor run? If it hums and the freezer stays warm, look at a defrost failure blocking airflow on an upright, a leaking seal, heat around the cabinet, or a refrigerant leak or restriction. If it's silent or only clicks, the start relay, overload or thermostat comes first.

A technician narrows it down by ear and by touch. A compressor that runs steadily while the cabinet warms points away from the electrical parts and toward the sealed system. One that tries, clicks off and tries again points to the relay or overload. On an upright, the fan and the frost pattern show whether air is moving.

Opening the refrigerant circuit requires EPA Section 608 certification, and leaks take time to find, so a freezer not freezing repair call starts by ruling out the simpler parts.

## Read your freezer before you call

Note what you find:

- **The sound.** Steady hum, silence, or a click every few minutes with no hum after it.
- **The frost.** Even frost on a chest freezer's walls, thick ice near the lid, frost on an upright's shelves, or a solid block on the back wall.
- **The outside of the cabinet.** Chest freezers usually run condenser tubing inside the outer walls, so warm sides are expected. Sweating along the lid, or one spot that's hot to the touch, can mean a gasket or airflow problem.
- **The gasket.** Close the lid or door on a dollar bill. If it slides out with no drag, that stretch isn't sealing.
- **The temperature.** An inexpensive appliance thermometer is the only reliable way to know. You want 0°F.

Two minutes of notes beats "it's just not cold" on the phone.

## Freezer frosting up: normal ice or a broken defrost?

On a manual defrost chest freezer, some frost is normal. Humid Central Texas air comes in with every opening and freezes onto the walls. Past about a quarter inch, that ice insulates the walls and makes the compressor work harder. Thick ice right along the top edge usually means the lid gasket is leaking.

A frost-free upright shouldn't build frost at all. Frost on the shelves, ice on the packages or a solid block behind the back panel points to the defrost heater, the defrost thermostat or the control board. That's freezer frost buildup repair, and chipping the ice away only buys a few weeks.

To defrost a chest freezer safely:

1. Move the food into coolers or a second freezer, and unplug the unit.
2. Open the drain plug at the bottom front, if it has one, with a pan or hose underneath.
3. Prop the lid open and set pans of hot water inside.
4. Once the ice loosens, lift sheets out by hand or with a plastic scraper.
5. Dry the inside, plug it back in and wait until it's cold before loading food.

No knives, screwdrivers or ice picks. One slip can puncture a refrigerant line hidden in the wall, and that ends most chest freezers. Skip heat guns and hair dryers too; they warp the liner.

## Freezer running constantly? Some causes you can check

**Worth checking yourself:**
- **The condenser.** Where there are coils or a grille at the back or bottom, vacuum off the dust and lint.
- **The seal.** Run the dollar-bill test all the way around.
- **The load.** A freezer just filled with warm food runs for hours, and that's normal.
- **The room.** A garage over 100°F keeps any freezer running longer.

**Needs a technician:** a thermostat that no longer shuts the compressor off, a failing fan, a defrost fault on an upright, or a low refrigerant charge from a leak. If it runs nonstop and still won't reach 0°F after you've ruled out the first four, call.

## What happens when a technician comes to look at the freezer?

Most freezer visits start with a path. A chest freezer at the back of a garage or shop is usually buried behind bikes, coolers and the Christmas tree box. ## Is the freezer worth fixing?

**Small, inexpensive chest freezer with a sealed system problem.** Usually not. Finding the leak, brazing and recharging can cost close to a new unit. An opinion from someone who has hauled plenty of these out of hot garages: a small chest freezer with a sealed system failure is rarely worth saving.

**Any freezer with a bad relay, thermostat or gasket.** Usually yes. Common parts.

**Large upright with a defrost or fan failure.** Often worth it. Uprights cost more to replace, and those are routine repairs.

**Older large freezer with a sealed system failure.** It depends on age, size and condition. Compare the price with a new one of the same size.

## Freezer repair FAQ

### Can a freezer lie on its side during a move?

Upright is best. If it has to lie down, many manufacturers suggest standing it upright for at least as long as it was on its side, or a full day, before plugging it in. That lets the compressor oil settle back. Check your manual, since advice varies by model.

### How long after plugging in should a freezer reach 0°F?

Plan on several hours, and up to a day for a large or empty unit. Keep the lid or door closed and load food only once it's cold. If it still isn't near 0°F after a full day, something is wrong. Trust the thermometer, not the dial.

### Can I keep a regular freezer in an unheated garage in winter?

Sometimes, but check its rating first. Some freezers stop running when the room gets too cold, because the thermostat thinks the job is done. Garage-ready models are built for those swings. If yours is rated for indoor use, keep a thermometer inside and check it during a cold snap.

### Should I unplug a chest freezer to defrost it, and how long does it take?

Yes, always unplug it first. With the lid open and pans of hot water inside, a heavy layer of ice often loosens in a few hours, and thin frost goes faster. Start on a cool morning so your coolers hold the food longer.

### Why does my freezer click, go quiet, then click again?

Usually the compressor can't get going, and its overload cuts the power to protect it, then lets it try again. A worn start relay is a common cause; a failing compressor is the less welcome one. A technician can test which, so call before the food softens.
