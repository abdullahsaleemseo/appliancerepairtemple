## Why opening a microwave isn't a DIY job

The high-voltage capacitor in a microwave can hold a dangerous charge even after the unit has been unplugged, sometimes for a long time. The high-voltage parts behind the cabinet, including the transformer, diode and capacitor, operate at thousands of volts. Discharging and testing them safely takes the right tools and training. Cleaning the inside, replacing a turntable or a bulb from the outside, and checking the breaker are fine. Taking the outer cabinet off isn't.

## Why is my microwave running but not heating?

The light comes on, the turntable spins and the fan runs, but the food stays cold. That pattern points to the high-voltage circuit, which makes the microwaves themselves. The usual failures are:

- **The high-voltage diode,** a small part that often fails and is among the less expensive fixes.
- **The magnetron,** the tube that actually produces the microwaves; it's the costly part.
- **The high-voltage capacitor,** which works with the diode to power the magnetron.
- **A door interlock switch,** one of several switches that confirm the door is closed. A weak one can let the microwave run without heating, or stop it from starting at all.

Thermal cutouts and fuses can cut the heat as well. Which of these failed decides whether microwave not heating repair makes sense on your unit, which is why the part matters more than the symptom. If you'd like to check the simple things first, the guide to [a microwave that runs but won't heat](https://appliancerepairtemple.com/blog/microwave-not-heating/) covers the safe outside checks, like the door latch and the power setting.

## Microwave turntable not spinning?

This one is often a quick fix. Lift out the glass tray and the roller ring underneath, wipe away food or spills, and make sure the ring's wheels turn freely and the tray seats on the coupler, the three-pronged piece in the center. A cracked coupler or a dirty roller ring stops the tray.

If the tray and ring are fine and the coupler still doesn't turn, the turntable motor under the floor has likely failed. On many models, a turntable on/off button gets pressed by accident too, so check that before anything else.

## Microwave sparking? Stop using it

Sparks or arcing inside a microwave are a stop-now sign. The common causes are metal in the oven (a twist tie, foil, a utensil, gold trim on a plate), a damaged waveguide cover and chipped or burned paint inside the cavity.

The waveguide cover is a small panel, often mica, on the inside wall where the microwaves enter. Grease and food splatter soak into it, and once it burns through, it sparks. On many models it's an inexpensive part that can be replaced. Chipped interior paint can arc too, and small spots can sometimes be touched up with microwave-safe cavity paint. If sparking continues with nothing metal inside and a clean cover, stop using the microwave and have it checked, because the problem may be inside the cabinet.

## Over-the-range microwaves do more than heat food

An over-the-range unit is also the vent hood for your stove, with an exhaust fan, grease filters and a cooktop light. When one fails, cooking gets harder in more ways than reheating leftovers, which is a big part of why repairs are usually worth it. Vent fans that stop or rattle, cooktop lights that burn out, and charcoal or grease filters that need replacing are common, and some of those don't require opening the cabinet at all.

Replacing one isn't a simple swap either. The microwave hangs from a mounting plate on the wall and bolts through the cabinet above. A new unit often needs a different plate, and lifting it into place is a two-person job.

Brands matter here too. On some [Samsung over-the-range models](https://appliancerepairtemple.com/brands/samsung-appliance-repair-temple-tx/), a code like SE points to a stuck or shorted keypad rather than the heating circuit, and some [LG microwaves](https://appliancerepairtemple.com/brands/lg-appliance-repair-temple-tx/) use inverter power instead of a traditional transformer, which changes how the heating side is diagnosed. Check any code against your manual.

## Keypad or door problems

Keypads that ignore some buttons, or register presses you didn't make, usually mean the touchpad membrane or the control board has failed, often after steam or splatter got behind the panel. A microwave that won't start with the door shut, or starts and stops when the door is bumped, points to a door switch or a worn latch. Door switches are inexpensive, but they're safety parts and need to be installed and tested correctly.

## Microwave repair FAQ

### Can a microwave leak radiation if the door is damaged?

A bent door, broken hinge, damaged seal or faulty latch can affect how well the door seals. If the door doesn't close properly or the latch feels loose, stop using the microwave and have it checked or replaced. The FDA sets leakage limits, and door damage is the main risk.

### Why does my microwave make a loud humming or buzzing noise?

Microwaves hum normally while running. A much louder hum or buzz, especially with no heating, often points to a failing magnetron, diode or capacitor. A grinding noise usually means the turntable motor or a stuck roller ring. Stop using it if the noise comes with a burning smell.

### Why does my over-the-range microwave fan turn on by itself?

Many over-the-range models have a heat sensor that turns the vent fan on automatically when the cooktop below gets hot, to protect the microwave's electronics. That's normal, even with the microwave off. If the fan runs constantly with the stove cold, the sensor or control may be faulty.

### Is it safe to run a microwave empty?

No. With nothing to absorb the energy, it reflects back toward the magnetron and can damage it. If you need to test a microwave, heat a cup of water instead. Running it empty for a few seconds by accident usually isn't harmful, but don't make a habit of it.

### How long do microwaves usually last?

Many last somewhere around seven to ten years, depending on use and care. Keeping the inside clean, especially the waveguide cover, and not running it empty help it last longer. Over-the-range units that also work as a vent hood see heavier use.
