## Above the tool

### Intro (hero)

Most appliance trouble never shows a code. A fridge that runs warm, a dryer that takes two cycles, a dishwasher that leaves grit behind: the machine knows something's off, but it isn't saying what. Pick the appliance, the main symptom, anything else you've noticed, its age and how it's running right now, and the checker ranks up to three likely causes from your answers, with the reason behind each one.

### Describe it like this

| Vague | Useful |
|---|---|
| "The fridge isn't working." | "Freezer's fine, the fridge side is warm, and the back of the freezer is white with frost." |
| "The dryer's broken." | "It tumbles and the timer runs, but the clothes come out cold." |
| "The washer's making a noise." | "Loud banging during spin, only with towels." |

The more specific the symptom, the more useful the ranking.

## Below the tool

### How the ranking moves: one example

Say the fresh-food side of your fridge is warm. On its own, that symptom has a handful of usual suspects: dusty condenser coils, a stalled fan, a defrost fault, a stuck damper. Now add a second sign, frost coating the back of the freezer. A defrost fault explains both, so it jumps to the top. Add that the fridge is twelve years old, and worn parts such as the start relay and door gaskets climb the list too. The result shows which answer moved each cause, so you can see the reasoning instead of taking it on faith. The checks for that exact pattern are covered in [why a refrigerator warms up](https://appliancerepairtemple.com/refrigerator-repair-temple-tx/).

### The four urgency labels

- **Monitor:** safe to keep using while you watch for changes.
- **Schedule soon:** plan a repair before the problem grows.
- **Get help today:** food, water damage or daily use is at stake. A warm fridge or a washer leaking onto the floor shouldn't wait for the weekend.
- **Stop use now:** switch it off and leave it off until a technician has checked it.

### Where to go next

### What the checker won't do

It only suggests checks you can do with the power off: cleaning a filter, looking at a hose, listening for a fan. It never asks you to open a sealed system, a gas line or an electrical panel. Confirming the fault means testing parts, and that's a technician's job. If you choose "unsafe signs present", it skips the causes and shows safety steps instead.

### Closing call to action

## FAQ (rewritten; none of these questions appear elsewhere on the site)

### What if my symptom isn't in the list?

Pick the closest one and add the rest in the other-symptoms step. If nothing comes close, skip the checker and call with a one-line description: what it's doing, when it started, and anything that changed recently, like a move or a power outage. That description is usually enough to point a technician in the right direction.

### Two things are wrong at once. Which do I pick first?

Pick the one that bothers you most as the main symptom and add the other as an extra sign. The checker gives more weight to causes that explain both, which is often how the real fault shows itself. A warm fridge plus water under the crisper drawers, for example, points straight at the defrost system.

### Why does the checker ask how it's running right now?

Because "runs but poorly" and "won't start at all" point to different parts. A fridge that hums and stays warm is a different job from one that's silent. It's also where you flag anything unsafe, which switches the result to safety steps instead of causes.

### Can the checker tell me whether it's worth fixing?

It adds a short repair-or-replace note based on the appliance's age, but it doesn't know the price of the part. For a fuller picture, run the same appliance and problem through the cost estimator, then ask a technician for the real figure once the fault is confirmed.
