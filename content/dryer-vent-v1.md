## Where does your vent go, and why does that matter?

| Vent route | Why it clogs | What cleaning involves |
|---|---|---|
| Short run straight through an outside wall | Little to slow the air down, so it clogs slowly, mostly at the outside hood | Brushing the short duct from both ends and clearing the hood |
| Long run through an attic or between floors | Air loses speed over distance and at every elbow, so lint drops out along the way | Running a rotary brush and vacuum through the full length, often from both ends, and checking joints and sagging sections |
| Roof vent | Lint has to climb straight up, falls back, and collects at the cap; caps attract nests | Cleaning from below and at the roof cap, which means safe roof access |
| Crushed transition duct behind the dryer | The flexible piece between the dryer and the wall gets flattened when the dryer is pushed back | Replacing it with a short rigid or semi-rigid metal connector and setting the dryer with space behind it |
| Vent cap with a stuck flap or a screen | A flap that barely opens or a screen over the opening traps lint right at the exit | Clearing or replacing the cap; screens should come off, since dryer caps aren't meant to have them |

## Signs your dryer vent is clogged

Clogged vents usually show it in time first: a load that used to dry in one cycle now needs a second, or clothes come out hot to the touch but still damp at the seams. Hot and damp together is the giveaway, because the heat is working but the moist air has nowhere to go.

Inside the house, if the top of the dryer gets hot, or the laundry room turns warm and muggy whenever it runs, air that should be leaving the house is backing up instead. A burning smell, even faint, means stop and look.

Outside, watch the vent hood while the dryer runs. Lint caught around the opening, or a flap that barely lifts, points to a restriction somewhere along the route. Some dryers now flag this on their own. Many LG models, for instance, measure airflow and show a vent-blocked warning, and [LG's Flow Sense codes](https://appliancerepairtemple.com/brands/lg-appliance-repair-temple-tx/) step up as the restriction gets worse.

## Is a clogged dryer vent a fire risk?

Yes. Lint is fine, dry and burns easily, and a restricted vent makes the dryer run hotter for longer. Failure to clean the dryer and its vent is a recognised factor in home dryer fires, which is the main reason vent cleaning is worth doing on a schedule rather than waiting for slow drying to force the issue.

A high-limit thermostat and a thermal fuse cut the heat when the exhaust runs too hot, and with a vent that stays clogged, a blown thermal fuse is a common result. If a cleaning doesn't bring drying times back, the dryer itself may need a look, and [why a dryer stops heating](https://appliancerepairtemple.com/dryer-repair-temple-tx/) covers the parts that usually fail.

## What a cleaning visit usually involves

**Behind the dryer.** The dryer gets pulled out and disconnected, and the transition duct is checked for crushing, tears and lint packed inside. Foil or vinyl connectors usually get swapped for metal.

**Along the run.** A rotary brush on flexible rods is fed through the duct while a vacuum pulls the loosened lint out. Long runs often get done from both ends.

**At the outside cap.** The hood is cleared, the flap is checked to make sure it swings freely, and any screen is removed. Roof caps get the same attention from above.

**Back at the dryer.** Once everything is reconnected, the dryer runs while airflow is checked at the outside hood, so you can feel the difference.

Ask when you call what the visit will include for your vent route, especially for a roof vent.

## Foil, vinyl or metal: what should the duct be?

Rigid or semi-rigid metal duct is the standard recommendation. Foil and vinyl flex ducts crush easily, trap lint in their ribs and sag between supports, which is why many dryer manufacturers and building codes discourage them.

Length matters as much as material. Residential codes based on the International Residential Code generally cap dryer duct length and subtract a set amount for each elbow, unless the dryer manufacturer allows a longer run. The exact allowance depends on your dryer, so check the installation section of its manual. Longer runs sometimes use a dryer booster fan.

## How often should a dryer vent be cleaned?

At least once a year is the common guidance, and [how often to clean a dryer vent](https://appliancerepairtemple.com/blog/how-often-clean-dryer-vent/) in your home comes down to how much laundry you run, how long the vent is and whether pets shed. Clean it sooner with several loads a day or a roof vent. A vent that clogs again quickly usually has a sag or a crushed connector somewhere along the route.

## Dryer vent FAQ

### Can I clean my dryer vent myself?

Often, yes, on a short, accessible run. Unplug the dryer (and shut the gas valve on a gas dryer), disconnect the duct, and use a dryer vent brush kit from both ends. Long attic runs, roof vents and ducts that have come apart are harder to do well and safely.

### Is a dryer vent the same as an air duct?

No. Air ducts move heated and cooled air through the house for the HVAC system. The dryer vent is a separate, single duct that carries hot, moist, lint-filled exhaust from the dryer to the outside. Cleaning one doesn't clean the other.

### Does a clogged vent raise my electric or gas bill?

It can. A restricted vent makes each load run longer, sometimes for two cycles instead of one, so the dryer uses more energy per load. Restoring good airflow usually brings drying times, and that extra energy use, back down to where they were.

### Can a dryer vent end in the attic or garage instead of outside?

It shouldn't. Dryer exhaust carries moisture and lint, and venting it indoors or into an attic can cause mold, damage and a fire risk. A gas dryer also produces combustion gases. Dryer vents are meant to end outside the house, with a proper cap.

### How do I keep birds out of the outside vent?

Use a cap made for dryer vents, with a flap or louvers that close when the dryer is off. Avoid adding a fine screen over the opening, since it traps lint and blocks airflow. Check the cap each spring, when birds tend to nest.
