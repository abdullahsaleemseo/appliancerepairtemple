## Lake houses that sit empty

Plenty of homes here are weekend places, second homes or rentals between guests. An appliance in a house nobody's in can fail on a Tuesday and stay failed until Friday evening, when someone opens the fridge to a smell or steps into a puddle by the washer. The repair itself is often ordinary. What makes it expensive is the time it went unnoticed: spoiled food, a soaked subfloor, ice maker water running into a cabinet for three days.

So the most useful thing on this page isn't a repair. It's the ten minutes before you lock the door.

## Before you leave the lake house

- **Water supply valves:** close the valves behind the washer and, if you'll be gone more than a few days, the one feeding the refrigerator's ice maker. For a longer absence, close the main and leave the water heater on vacation mode.
- **Ice maker:** switch it off or raise the arm, and empty the bin so old ice doesn't fuse into a block or melt if the power drops.
- **Fridge temperature:** check the fresh-food side reads about 37°F and the freezer about 0°F before leaving, so you know it was right when you left.
- **Freezer thermometer:** leave a simple dial thermometer inside the freezer. Some owners also freeze a cup of water, set a coin on top, and leave it in; if the coin has sunk to the bottom when you return, the freezer thawed and refroze while you were away.
- **Dryer lint:** clean the lint screen after the last load and don't leave the dryer running when you head out.
- **Dishwasher and washer doors:** leave them cracked open so seals dry and nothing smells sour on return.

## Arriving to a problem

Walk in, find a warm fridge or water on the floor, and the instinct is to start fixing. Slow down for two minutes first.

Photograph what you see before you touch anything: the temperature display, any code, the water on the floor and where it seems to start, and the model tag if you can reach it. Then shut off the water at the valve behind the appliance, or the main if you can't tell where it's coming from. Check whether the power went out while you were away; clocks blinking on the microwave or range are a giveaway, and a tripped breaker or GFCI outlet in the garage is a common reason a second fridge or freezer goes quiet.

Once (254) 615-2088 picks up, open with four things: how long the house sat empty, whether the power seems to have gone out, what the appliance is doing now, and the brand and model from the photo. That history often explains the failure faster than the appliance does. If food has thawed, toss what's spoiled rather than refreezing it; a refrigerator that's warm only because a door didn't seal needs a different repair from one whose cooling stopped.

## Refrigerators, freezers and ice makers at the lake

Lake homes tend to have more cold storage than everyday houses: a second fridge in the garage, a chest freezer for the catch, an ice maker that works overtime on summer weekends and then sits idle. ## Outdoor kitchens and beverage coolers

## Morgan's Point Resort FAQ

### Should I leave the fridge running at the lake house while I'm gone?

For a few weeks, yes: leave it running at its normal setting with the doors shut. For a long absence, many owners empty it, unplug it, and prop the doors open so it doesn't grow mold. Either way, turn off the ice maker and its water supply.

### Can a technician come if I'm not at the lake house?

Ask when you call. Access comes down to the schedule and who can open the house, such as a neighbor, caretaker or property manager. Make sure you can be reached by phone so you can approve any repair before it's done.

### My address says Belton, but I'm in Morgan's Point Resort. Does that matter?

Not for coverage; both share 76513. Just say Morgan's Point Resort and give the street, so the visit is routed to the lake side and not into town.
