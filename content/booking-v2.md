# Book an Appliance Repair in Temple, TX

Booking takes one short phone call. Call us at (254) 615-2088, tell us what's wrong, and we'll work out the visit from there.

**[Call (254) 615-2088]** (tracked call button)

Leak or gas smell? Handle that before anything else: shut the water valve for a leak; for gas, get everyone out and ring 911 or the gas utility from the yard. Then call and say it's urgent.

## Have this ready

Five minutes with this list shortens the call and gives the technician a head start on parts.

- **The appliance and its brand.** "Whirlpool top-load washer" or "Samsung French door fridge" is plenty.
- **The model number.** It's on a sticker or metal plate, and each appliance hides it somewhere different:
  - Refrigerator: on an inner wall of the fresh-food side, often near the light housing.
  - Chest freezer: on the outside back or side, or under the lid rim.
  - Top-load washer: beneath the lid near the hinge, or on the rear of the console. Front-load washer and dryer: on the rim of the door opening.
  - Dishwasher: on the edge of the door or the tub, visible with the door open.
  - Range or wall oven: behind the bottom drawer, or on the frame with the oven door open.
  - Over-the-range microwave: on the frame inside the door.
  A phone photo of the tag is better than copying it by hand.
- **What it's doing.** One or two sentences: won't cool, won't drain, clicks but won't light, leaks from the front. Say when it started.
- **Any error code.** Write it exactly as the display shows it. You can look it up first in [the error code decoder for major brands](https://appliancerepairtemple.com/appliance-error-code-decoder/); some codes point to a filter or a door you can sort out yourself.
- **Your street and ZIP.** We cover Temple and the towns around it, and the street matters as much as the ZIP when a code like 76513 spans more than one town. Each town appears on [the list of towns we cover](https://appliancerepairtemple.com/service-areas/).
- **Access notes.** Gate codes, pets, the best door, where the appliance sits (garage, shop, upstairs) and who'll be there.

## What happens after you call

1. **You describe the problem.** Which appliance, whose brand, the model number and the symptom. If a quick check at home might solve it, a reset breaker or a straightened hose, you'll hear that first.
2. **You ask what you want to know.** What the trip costs, how the repair figure gets set and when someone can come are all fair questions before you commit.
3. **A technician comes out.** They look at the appliance, find what failed and explain it in plain words.
4. **You get the price, then decide.** Nothing is replaced until you've agreed to the cost. When the fix would cost more than an old appliance deserves, that gets said plainly as well.
5. **The repair, or the part.** Some repairs are finished during the same visit. If a part has to be ordered, you'll know which one and how the return visit works.

## A few things worth knowing

Renting? Check your lease or ask your landlord first, since they usually arrange appliance repairs. New build, under a year old? The builder or the manufacturer may cover an installation fault. Other common questions, from cost to what to keep after the visit, are answered in [the appliance repair FAQ](https://appliancerepairtemple.com/faq/), and [who we are and how we work](https://appliancerepairtemple.com/about/) covers the rest.
