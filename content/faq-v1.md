# Temple Appliance Repair FAQ

These are the questions Temple homeowners ask before, during and after an appliance repair, answered plainly. Anything that depends on your appliance or the day's schedule, like the visit fee or the next opening, gets answered best on the phone at (254) 615-2088.

## Booking a repair

### Can I book online instead of calling?

Yes. Use [the online repair request](https://appliancerepairtemple.com/get-connected/) to send your address, the appliance and the symptom; someone follows up from there. A phone call is quicker if the appliance is leaking or food is at risk.

### What if I need to change or cancel the appointment?

Call as soon as you know. Ask how changes are handled when you book, so there are no surprises later.

### Does someone have to be home for the visit?

Usually an adult needs to be there to let the technician in, show where the appliance is and approve the repair once the problem is found. If that's hard to arrange, say so when you book and ask what's possible.

### Which towns are covered?

Temple, the smaller Bell County towns nearby, and Moody just past the county line. Every town and ZIP is listed on [the service area map](https://appliancerepairtemple.com/service-areas/).

## The visit itself

### Do I need to empty the refrigerator before the technician comes?

Not completely, but clear the shelves near the back wall and the freezer's rear panel, since that's where fans and defrost parts sit. Keep a cooler ready in case the repair takes a while.

### Will the technician need to move the appliance?

Often, yes. Washers, dryers, ranges and refrigerators usually have to be pulled out to reach the back panel, hoses or vent. Clear a path and a little floor space in front, and mention fragile flooring when you book.

### What if it can't be fixed on the first visit?

Sometimes the part has to be ordered. In that case the first visit pins down the failure and the part number, and the repair is finished when the part arrives. Ask up front how a second trip is handled and charged.

## Cost and payment

### How is the price worked out?

There are usually two pieces: the visit, which covers coming out and finding the problem, and the repair itself, parts plus labor. Ask what the visit costs before booking, and hear the repair figure before a single part comes out.

### What if the appliance isn't worth fixing?

You may still owe the visit, since the diagnosis was done. Ask about that before booking. A good technician tells you plainly when a repair costs too much for the appliance's age, and you decide.

### How do I pay, and is the repair covered afterward?

Payment methods and any warranty on parts and labor are set out when you book or before work begins. Ask about both on the call and write the answers down.

Got a cost question about your own appliance? Phone (254) 615-2088; share the brand, the tag's model number and the symptom, and the answer will fit your repair instead of being a guess.

## Repair or replace

### At what point is an appliance not worth fixing?

When the repair costs a large share of a new one and the appliance is well into its life, replacement usually wins. On an older unit, a compressor or the central circuit board is where most people draw the line. The full set of factors is in [the guide to repairing or replacing an appliance](https://appliancerepairtemple.com/blog/repair-or-replace-appliance/).

### My appliance shows an error code. Should I look it up first?

Yes. Some codes point to something you can clear yourself, like a clogged filter or a door not fully shut. Run it through [the error code lookup tool](https://appliancerepairtemple.com/appliance-error-code-decoder/), and write the code down exactly before you reset anything.

### Can an appliance I bought used be repaired?

Usually, yes. Copy the model and serial from its tag so parts can be matched, and ask the previous owner whether it had any repairs.

## Safety: when to stop using it

### Which warning signs mean I should switch it off now?

Turn it off and leave it off if you smell gas, see smoke or scorching, hear sparking, feel a cord or outlet that's hot, or find water spreading toward an outlet. With a gas smell, leave the house with everyone and phone the gas or propane company, or 911, once you're out.

### Should I shut off the breaker before the technician arrives?

Only if the appliance is sparking, smoking or tripping the breaker repeatedly. Otherwise leave it on so the technician can see the fault happen.

### Is a cracked or damaged power cord a repair or a replacement?

Usually a repair: many appliance cords and plugs can be replaced on their own. Stop using the appliance until it's fixed, and don't tape over the damage.
