## Above the tool

### Intro (hero)

A few minutes of upkeep heads off a lot of repair calls. Pick the appliances in your house, say how old they are and how hard they work, and the generator builds a checklist sorted into monthly, seasonal, yearly and professional tasks. Homes on hard water, with pets or with appliances in a hot garage get shorter intervals where it counts.

### Decide these three before you start

- **Is your water hard?** Most of the water around Temple is. Tick it, and dishwasher, washer and ice maker tasks come around more often, because scale is what clogs their screens and valves.
- **Any pets?** Pet hair finds its way into condenser coils and dryer lint screens fast. Tick it, and coil cleaning moves up.
- **Where does each appliance live?** A fridge in an air-conditioned kitchen and one in a Central Texas garage need very different attention. The placement question for each appliance takes care of that.

## Below the tool

### Reading your plan: the four buckets

- **Monthly.** The quick ones: wiping the washer door gasket, rinsing the dishwasher filter, checking the fridge and freezer temperatures. A few minutes each.
- **Seasonal.** Every few months: vacuuming the condenser coils, running a washer cleaning cycle, checking the dryer vent flap outside while it runs. Before July is a good time for the coils.
- **Yearly.** Replacing worn washer fill hoses, checking the range's anti-tip bracket, descaling the dishwasher on hard water.
- **Professional.** Anything involving gas, sealed refrigeration or wiring, plus a full dryer vent cleaning through walls or the attic. These are marked so you don't try them yourself; the long vent runs are covered in [dryer vent cleaning for Temple homes](https://appliancerepairtemple.com/dryer-vent-cleaning-temple-tx/).

### Habits that prevent most repair calls

- Clean the dryer lint screen every load, not every few loads.
- Vacuum the fridge's condenser coils before summer, and again in fall if you have pets.
- Swap rubber washer hoses for braided ones before they bulge.

### When maintenance turns into a repair

Upkeep keeps things running; it doesn't fix a part that has already failed. A fridge that stays warm after the coils are clean, a dryer still taking two cycles after the vent is cleared, a dishwasher holding water with a clean filter: those are repair calls. If you're not sure which one you've got, describe it in [the symptom checker](https://appliancerepairtemple.com/appliance-symptom-diagnostic-tool/) first.

### Saving and printing

Tick tasks off as you go, copy the list into your notes, or use Print / Save as PDF and choose "Save as PDF" as the printer. Only the checklist prints. Your answers stay in this browser tab and nothing is sent anywhere.

### Safety

Unplug the appliance or switch off its breaker before any cleaning or inspection. Tasks marked professional involve gas, sealed refrigeration or wiring, and they belong to a technician.

## FAQ (rewritten; none of these questions appear elsewhere on the site)

### Which maintenance jobs should I leave to a technician?

Anything that involves gas, sealed refrigeration or wiring, and a full dryer vent cleaning through walls or the attic. The checklist marks those as professional. Everything else on it, from rinsing filters to vacuuming coils, is meant for you to do with the appliance unplugged and a few basic tools.

### Does hard water really change the schedule that much?

Yes. Minerals coat dishwasher spray arms, washer and ice maker inlet screens, and heating elements. On hard water, cleaning and descaling more often keeps the water flowing and the heat where it should be. Ticking hard or well water shortens those tasks automatically.

### I look after a few rentals. Can I make one plan for all of them?

Run the generator once per home, since each one has its own appliances, ages and conditions, and print or save each plan. Taping one inside a cabinet door in each unit gives tenants the monthly tasks and keeps the professional ones on your list.

### Will regular maintenance void my appliance's warranty?

No. Cleaning filters, coils and lint screens is exactly what the owner's manual asks you to do. Leave anything marked professional to a qualified technician, and check the manual or the maker's warranty terms before opening panels on a newer appliance.
