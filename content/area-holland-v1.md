## Old house, new appliance

Holland is a small town in southeast Bell County, with an old downtown and plenty of houses that have stood for generations. Those homes were wired and plumbed for the appliances of their day. A new dryer, range or refrigerator often expects something a little different: a four-prong cord, a grounded outlet, a modern gas connector, a supply line that isn't half rusted shut.

When the two don't match, it looks like a broken appliance: a new dryer whose cord won't fit the wall, a range that trips the breaker. Sorting out whether the problem belongs to the appliance, the wiring or the plumbing saves a wasted visit.

## The compatibility checklist

**Dryer cord and receptacle**
Look for: the number of slots in the wall outlet behind the dryer. Houses wired before the late 1990s usually have a three-slot dryer receptacle; newer ones have four, because the electrical code changed to require a separate ground. The dryer's cord has to match the outlet, and the bonding strap on the dryer's terminal block is set differently for three-wire and four-wire hookups.
Who fixes it: an appliance technician can fit the right cord and set the strap; changing the outlet itself, or adding a ground wire, is an electrician's job.

**Range cord and receptacle**
Look for: the same three-slot or four-slot question at the range outlet, plus scorch marks or a loose fit.
Who fixes it: the cord is appliance work; a burned or loose receptacle goes to an electrician.

**Grounded outlets for the fridge and dishwasher**
Look for: two-prong outlets with no ground hole. Refrigerators and other appliances with three-prong plugs need a grounded outlet; a cheater adapter doesn't provide one.
Who fixes it: an electrician.

**Gas shutoff valve and connector**
Look for: a shutoff valve you can reach and turn behind the range or dryer, and the connector between it and the appliance. Old uncoated brass connectors can crack with age and have drawn federal safety warnings; a new appliance deserves a new coated one.
Who fixes it: connector replacement at the appliance can be part of the install; gas line or valve work goes to a licensed plumber or your gas or propane company.

**Water supply lines**
Look for: rubber washer hoses that bulge or crack, a fridge ice maker fed by a self-piercing saddle valve, and old galvanized pipe at the valves that's hard to turn or weeps.
Who fixes it: hoses and the fridge line are appliance work; corroded valves and pipe go to a plumber.

**Floor under the washer**
Look for: a floor that flexes or slopes. Older wood floors can bounce under a modern washer's high spin speed, making it walk across the room or bang the wall.
Who fixes it: an appliance technician levels the washer's feet; a soft or sagging floor needs a carpenter.

## Holland FAQ

### My new dryer's cord doesn't fit the wall outlet. What now?

Don't force it or use an adapter. Check whether the outlet has three slots or four, then get the matching cord for the dryer and set its ground strap to match, following the installation guide. If you'd rather switch the outlet to four-prong, that's an electrician's job.

### Can I plug my refrigerator into a two-prong outlet with an adapter?

It'll run, but it isn't grounded, and that's a safety risk. Have an electrician install a grounded outlet for the fridge.
