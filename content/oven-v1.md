## Know your range: the parts that fail

| Part | Gas version | Electric version | Typical failure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cooktop burner | Burner head and cap, lit by a spark igniter | Coil element in a receptacle, or a radiant element under glass | Gas: clicks but won't light. Electric: one burner stays cold |
| Burner control | Gas valve and spark switch | Infinite switch that cycles the element | Burner stuck on high, or heat that won't adjust |
| Bake heat | Bake igniter (glow bar) and gas valve | Bake element, exposed or hidden under the oven floor | Oven won't heat or takes far too long |
| Broil heat | Broil burner at the top, on many models | Broil element at the top | Broil works but bake doesn't, or the reverse |
| Temperature sensor | Thin metal probe inside the oven cavity | Same | Oven runs hot, runs cold or shows a code |
| Controls and clock | Control board, keypad and relays | Same | Dead display, error codes, buttons that don't respond |

A technician usually starts with the symptom and the fuel. On a gas oven that won't heat, the first look is at the bake igniter: whether it glows, and how long it takes. On an electric range, the element gets a continuity test and the outlet gets checked for both legs of its 240-volt supply, since one tripped side of the breaker can leave the clock lit and the oven cold. The temperature sensor is measured against its expected resistance, and the control is checked for codes before any part is condemned.

## Cooktop {#cooktop}

### Gas stove won't light

A burner that clicks but won't catch is the most common gas cooktop call, and it's often not a broken part. After a spill or a cleaning, the burner cap may sit crooked, or the burner head may still be wet. Lift the cap, dry everything, set the cap back so it sits flat and centered, and try again. Food packed into the small holes around the burner head blocks the flame too; a straight pin clears them.

If the clicking never stops even with the knob off, the spark switch behind that knob is usually wet or worn. If one burner won't click at all while the others do, the spark electrode or its wire is the suspect. Gas stove repair in Temple is mostly these small parts, plus the occasional gas valve, and stove repair in Temple, TX on electric cooktops is just as often a single switch or element.

### Electric burner not working

With a coil cooktop, swap the cold element into a working burner's spot. If it heats there, the element is fine and the receptacle it plugs into, or the infinite switch behind the knob, is the problem. Receptacles burn out when an element sits loose for a long time. On a smooth glass top, the radiant element sits under the glass, so a cold zone usually means the element or its switch has failed; a cracked glass top is a separate and bigger repair. There's a step-by-step for [a stove burner that's stopped working](https://appliancerepairtemple.com/blog/stove-burner-not-working/) that covers the swap test and the checks for gas burners. Cooktop repair on either fuel is usually a single part.

## Oven {#oven}

### Why is my oven not heating?

Gas ovens usually point to the bake igniter. It's a glow bar that has to draw enough current to open the gas valve. As it ages, it still glows orange but no longer draws enough, so the valve never opens and the oven sits cold or takes forever to heat. Watch through the window or the broiler drawer: a glow with no flame after a minute or so points straight to a weak igniter.

An electric oven's failed bake element often shows a break, blister or bright spot when it fails, though many newer ovens hide it under the floor panel. [Whirlpool ovens](https://appliancerepairtemple.com/brands/whirlpool-appliance-repair-temple-tx/) are a common example of that hidden design, so an electric oven repair there starts with a meter rather than a look. If broil heats but bake doesn't, or the other way around, the element that isn't working is your answer.

### Oven temperature off?

Put an inexpensive oven thermometer on the middle rack, set the oven to 350°F and let it cycle for at least half an hour. If it reads within about 10 to 15 degrees, the oven is in normal range. If it's consistently off by more, many ovens have a calibration setting in the menu that offsets the temperature by a set number of degrees; the manual explains how to use it.

If the error is large or erratic, the temperature sensor is the usual suspect, and it's a straightforward part to swap. Some ranges report a failed sensor as a code. GE ranges, for example, use F-codes for sensor and control faults, and [GE's oven codes](https://appliancerepairtemple.com/brands/ge-appliance-repair-temple-tx/) point to the sensor more often than to the board. [Frigidaire ranges](https://appliancerepairtemple.com/brands/frigidaire-appliance-repair-temple-tx/) show F10 for an oven running hotter than it should on many models, so cut power to the range until it's looked at. Codes differ between models, so check yours against the manual.

### Wall ovens and double ovens

Wall ovens have the same heating parts as a range, but reaching them means sliding the oven out of the cabinet, which takes two people and some care with the wiring. Double ovens share one control board between both cavities, so a fault can show up in one oven or both. Wall oven repair is common and usually worth it, since replacing a built-in oven often means matching the cabinet cutout.

## Self-clean and door lock {#self-clean}

### Oven door won't unlock after self-clean

The door locks during self-clean because the oven gets far hotter than normal cooking, and it stays locked until the oven has cooled. First, wait. Give it a couple of hours after the cycle ends. If it's still locked when fully cool, turn the range off at the breaker for a minute or two and back on, which resets the lock on many models.

Never force the latch or pry the door. That bends the lock mechanism and can damage the hinges, turning a reset into a repair. If the lock motor or switch has failed, a technician can release it and replace the part.

Self-clean's extreme heat is also hard on the oven itself. It can blow a thermal fuse, leaving the oven dead, or stress the control board and door lock. That's worth remembering before the holidays.

## Oven and stove repair FAQ

### Is a little smoke normal when I use a new oven?

Yes, for the first few uses. Manufacturing oils burn off the elements and insulation, which can smoke and smell. Run the oven empty at a high temperature for an hour with the windows open. Smoke that continues after several uses, or comes from one spot, should be checked.

### Why does my gas oven make a whoosh or a small boom when it lights?

A soft whoosh as the gas lights is normal. A loud boom usually means gas built up before lighting, often because the igniter is weak and slow. Stop using the oven and have the igniter checked, since delayed ignition gets worse over time.

### Can I replace an oven bake element myself?

On many electric ovens with an exposed element, yes: turn off the breaker, remove two screws, pull the element forward and disconnect it. Hidden elements under the oven floor take more disassembly. Never work on a gas oven's igniter or valve without shutting off the gas and power.

### Why does my oven light or clock work but the oven won't heat?

On an electric range, half of the double breaker may have tripped, which keeps the clock on but cuts the 240 volts the elements need. On a gas range, the igniter or valve is the usual cause. Reset the breaker fully before calling.

### Is it safe to use the cooktop if the oven is broken?

Usually, yes, as long as there's no gas smell, burning smell or sparking. The cooktop and oven often run on separate parts. If the problem involves the control board or wiring, though, ask before using either part of the range.

### What does a blinking clock on my oven mean?

Usually a power interruption, such as a brief outage or a tripped breaker. Reset the clock and it should stop. If the clock keeps resetting by itself, or other functions act up with it, the control board or a loose connection may need a look.
