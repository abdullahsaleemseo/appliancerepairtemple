## Common appliance problems in Temple homes

A few problems come up again and again around here, and most of them trace back to the local climate, the water or the age of the house.

- **Refrigerators and garage freezers in the summer heat.** Long, hot summers make condenser coils and fans work overtime. Dust on the coils, a weak condenser fan or a tired compressor relay can turn a fridge that was barely keeping up in May into a warm one by July.
- **Hard water scale.** Water in much of the area is hard, and the minerals build up in washer inlet valves, dishwasher spray arms and refrigerator ice maker valves. The first sign is often a slow fill, weak spray or small, hollow ice cubes.
- **Lint in long dryer vents.** Many homes have dryer vents that run through an attic or along an outside wall for a long distance. Lint builds up in those runs, the dryer overheats or takes forever, and the thermal fuse eventually gives out.
- **Older wiring and gas connections.** In older homes near downtown, a new range or dryer sometimes meets an outlet, cord or gas connection that was never meant for it. If the connection looks unsafe, we will tell you so you can have an electrician or plumber take care of it.
- **Washers stuck before the spin.** An unbalanced load, a clogged drain pump filter or too many suds from regular detergent in a high-efficiency washer stop more spin cycles than broken motors do.

## Is it worth repairing an appliance?

Most of the time, yes. A repair usually makes sense when the appliance is in the first two thirds of its expected life and the repair costs less than about half the price of a comparable new unit.

Here is how that rule of thumb plays out. A six-year-old refrigerator with a failed evaporator fan is an easy call: the part is common, the repair is quick and the fridge has years left. A fourteen-year-old refrigerator that needs a new compressor is a different story; at that age, putting the money toward a new unit is often smarter. A dryer of almost any age with a broken belt or a blown thermal fuse is worth fixing, because those are some of the least expensive repairs in the house.

A technician will give you a straight answer for your appliance. For a deeper look at lifespans and the numbers behind the decision, read our guide on [whether to repair or replace an appliance](https://appliancerepairtemple.com/blog/repair-or-replace-appliance/).
