## Above the tool

### Intro (hero)

Before you call, get a feel for how big the job is. Pick the appliance and what it's doing, add the brand and its age, and the estimator shows the parts and labor a repair like that usually takes, plus a straight read on whether repairing still beats replacing. There are no dollar figures here: prices depend on the part, so the result comes back as a level you can ask about on the phone.

### Have these three answers ready

- **What it's doing.** Not "it's broken" but "runs, no heat" or "fills, won't drain." The closer the symptom, the closer the guide.
- **The brand.** Premium and built-in brands move the result, because parts cost more and removal takes longer.
- **Roughly how old it is.** The model tag or the purchase paperwork usually gives it away. Age decides a lot of repair-or-replace calls on its own.

## Below the tool

### Reading your result

The estimate lands on one of four levels:

| Level | What it usually means |
|---|---|
| Lower | A visit plus a small part or an adjustment, like a door switch, a fuse or a cleared drain. |
| Moderate | A common part and an hour or two of labor, such as a pump, a fan motor or an igniter. |
| Higher | A larger component or a longer job, like a control board or a drum bearing. |
| Major | A compressor, a sealed system or a main board. This is where repair or replace gets a serious look. |

Under the level, the estimator lists the adjustments it made and why: a premium brand, a built-in unit, an older appliance, an urgent problem. Read those reasons; they're often more useful than the level itself.

### The repair-or-replace line

The bar compares the top of the repair estimate with the cost of a comparable new appliance, then checks the age against a typical lifespan. Lean toward repair when the repair is a small share of replacement and the appliance has years left. Weigh both when it's in the middle. Look hard at replacing when the repair passes about half the cost of new on an appliance close to wearing out. The thinking behind that rule is explained in [our repair-or-replace walkthrough](https://appliancerepairtemple.com/blog/repair-or-replace-appliance/).

### What the estimator can't see

It doesn't know your exact model, whether a second part is also worn, or whether the symptom has a different cause than the one you picked. A code on the display narrows that down; run it through [the error code decoder](https://appliancerepairtemple.com/appliance-error-code-decoder/) first if you have one. The real price comes after a technician has looked at the appliance, and you agree to it before any work starts.

### Safety first

If there's a gas smell, smoke, sparking or a burning smell, the estimator shows safety steps instead of a cost guide. Leave a gas smell to the gas company or 911, called from outside.

### Closing call to action

## FAQ (rewritten in the live voice; not used anywhere else on the site)

### Why doesn't the estimator show dollar amounts?

Because the price turns on the exact part and how long the job takes, and a figure on a web page would only be a guess. The level tells you how big the repair usually is, so you know what to ask about. A technician gives the real price after finding the problem, before any work begins.

### Why does a built-in or premium appliance push the level up?

Two reasons: parts for premium brands cost more, and built-in units take extra time to slide out and refit. The flip side is that they also cost more to replace, so a repair that looks large can still be the better choice for a high-end appliance.

### Why does the estimator ask for my ZIP code?

To check that you're inside the area covered, Temple plus the nearby Bell County communities. A ZIP outside it is flagged and the result is treated as general guidance, since distance can change the visit. Ask about it when you call if you're near the edge.

### Is the result a quote?

No. It's a guide to the size of the job, built from the parts and labor a repair like yours usually takes. The actual price comes after a technician has looked at the appliance, and no part goes in until you say yes.
