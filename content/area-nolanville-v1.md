## Builder-grade, year one

Nolanville has grown with new subdivisions along the US-190 corridor between Belton and Harker Heights. New houses arrive with a full set of builder-installed appliances: dishwasher, range, over-the-range microwave, often a refrigerator, sometimes a washer and dryer. The appliances themselves are usually fine. What goes wrong in the first year tends to be the installation: a drain hose that was never connected right, a duct squashed behind the dryer, a fridge water line kinked when it was pushed into place.

That matters for two reasons. An installation fault may be the builder's to fix under the home's warranty, at no cost to you. And a repair technician called out for a "broken" dishwasher that's really a capped drain will find the problem in minutes, but you'll still pay for the visit. A few minutes with the list below sorts that out first.

## Dishwashers and disposals in new kitchens

A brand-new dishwasher that leaves water in the tub after every cycle is one of the classic first-month calls in a new build. Check the disposal knockout plug first, then the drain hose loop under the sink. If both are fine, the trouble is in the dishwasher itself, and [how dishwasher drain and fill problems get traced](https://appliancerepairtemple.com/dishwasher-repair-temple-tx/) walks through the rest. A disposal that hums but won't turn, or trips its reset button, has its own short list of fixes in [what to try when a disposal jams or hums](https://appliancerepairtemple.com/garbage-disposal-repair-temple-tx/).

## Dryers that take two cycles

In a new laundry room, a dryer that never seems to dry in one cycle usually isn't broken. The short transition duct behind it is crushed, or the builder used foil duct that sags and collects lint. Pull the dryer forward, straighten or replace the duct with semi-rigid metal, and try again. If the wall duct itself is long or lint-packed, [cleaning the full dryer vent run](https://appliancerepairtemple.com/dryer-vent-cleaning-temple-tx/) restores airflow and keeps the heat parts from failing early.

## Builder warranty, manufacturer warranty or a repair tech?

Three different parties can be responsible, and the order matters. The builder usually handles how things were installed, for a period set out in your closing paperwork. The appliance manufacturer handles defects in the appliance itself, for a set period that often starts at closing. A repair technician is the right call once those don't apply, or when you'd rather not wait. Keep the closing date and the appliance manuals together; we'll ask for the model number either way, so having it ready helps whoever you call.
